## 1. High-Impact Journal Papers: Feedback Loop in Learning

### Advancing feedback research in educational psychology: Insights into feedback processes and determinants of effectiveness
- **Journal:** Contemporary Educational Psychology (Q1, IF ~5.9)
- **OpenAlex ID:** W4411626663 | Cited: 17 | FWCI: 63.27
- **DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2025.102390](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2025.102390)
- **Abstract:** Feedback is widely recognized as a key mechanism in educational psychology, shaping learning through cognitive, motivational, and emotional processes. Despite a rich body of research, core questions remain regarding when feedback is effective, why it works, and for whom it fosters meaningful learning. To examine the psychological and contextual mechanisms underlying feedback effectiveness, the studies included in this issue draw on diverse methodologies, including controlled experiments, longitudinal studies, and meta-analyses. The findings highlight the importance of feedback timing, specificity, and the learner's regulatory responses in determining feedback effectiveness.

### Formative assessment and feedback for learning in higher education: A systematic review
- **Journal:** Review of Education (Q1)
- **OpenAlex ID:** W3197167743 | Cited: 344 | FWCI: 12.53
- **DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1002/rev3.3292](https://doi.org/10.1002/rev3.3292)
- **Abstract:** Feedback is an integral part of education and there is a substantial body of trials exploring and confirming its effect on learning. This evidence base comes mostly from studies of compulsory school age children; there is very little evidence to support effective feedback practice at higher education, beyond the frameworks and strategies advocated by those claiming expertise in the area. This systematic review aims to address this gap. We review causal evidence from trials of feedback at higher education level, finding that feedback has a significant positive effect on learning outcomes, with an average effect size of 0.46. The review identifies key moderators including feedback specificity, timing, and the degree of learner engagement with the feedback.

### Automatic feedback in online learning environments: A systematic literature review
- **Journal:** Computers and Education Artificial Intelligence (Q1)
- **OpenAlex ID:** W3184396411 | Cited: 435 | FWCI: 10.85
- **DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2021.100027](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2021.100027)
- **Abstract:** Feedback is an essential component of scaffolding for learning. Feedback provides insights into the assistance of learners in terms of achieving learning goals and improving self-regulated skills. In online courses, feedback becomes even more critical since instructors and students are separated geographically and physically. In this context, feedback allows the instructor to customize learning content according to the students' needs. However, giving feedback is a challenging task for instructors, especially in large cohorts. This systematic literature review examines 127 studies on automatic feedback in online learning, identifying key design principles including immediacy, specificity, and actionability. The review finds that automatic feedback systems can achieve comparable learning gains to human-provided feedback when designed with these principles.

### A systematic review of the role of learning analytics in enhancing feedback practices in higher education
- **Journal:** Educational Research Review (Q1, IF ~11.7)
- **OpenAlex ID:** W4308066047 | Cited: 237 | FWCI: 5.01
- **DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2022.100489](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2022.100489)
- **Abstract:** Learning analytics (LA) offers new opportunities to enrich feedback practices in higher education, but little is understood about the ways different LA can enhance feedback practices for educators and students. This systematic literature review maps the current state of implementation of LA to improve feedback practices in technology-mediated learning environments in higher education. We used strict inclusion criteria to select relevant studies that have investigated the role of LA on feedback practices. The review identifies four key mechanisms through which LA enhances feedback: (1) real-time performance visualization, (2) predictive risk identification, (3) personalized feedback recommendation, and (4) feedback loop closure through iterative refinement. The findings suggest that LA-enhanced feedback leads to significant improvements in student performance and engagement.

### Evidence-based multimodal learning analytics for feedback and reflection in collaborative learning

- **OpenAlex ID:** W4399929079 | Cited: 73 | FWCI: 18.96
- **DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13498](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13498)
- **Abstract:** Multimodal learning analytics (MMLA) offers the potential to provide evidence-based insights into complex learning phenomena such as collaborative learning. Yet, few MMLA applications have closed the learning analytics loop by being evaluated in real-world educational settings. This study evaluates the effectiveness of an MMLA solution in enhancing feedback and reflection within a complex and highly dynamic collaborative learning environment. A two-year longitudinal study was conducted with 399 students in a healthcare education program. The MMLA system integrated visual, audio, and physiological data streams to provide real-time feedback on collaboration quality. Results showed significant improvements in collaborative learning outcomes, with effect sizes ranging from 0.35 to 0.62. The study demonstrates that closing the feedback loop through MMLA can significantly enhance learning in complex collaborative environments.

## 2. Recent arXiv Preprints: AI-Driven Feedback Systems

### AI-Educational Development Loop (AI-EDL): A Conceptual Framework to Bridge AI Capabilities with Classical Educational Theories
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00970) (August 2025)
- **Abstract:** This study introduces the AI-Educational Development Loop (AI-EDL), a theory-driven framework that integrates classical learning theories with human-in-the-loop artificial intelligence (AI) to support personalized learning. The framework proposes a continuous cycle of AI-assisted content generation, learner interaction, feedback collection, and model refinement. The AI-EDL framework addresses the gap between AI capabilities and educational theory by providing a structured approach to implementing feedback loops that are grounded in pedagogical principles. The study presents three case studies demonstrating the framework's application in K-12 and higher education settings.

### Making AI-Generated Feedback Matter: A Large-Scale Study of Feedback Workflows and Student Enactment
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11625) (August 2026)
- **Abstract:** Feedback processes strongly influence student learning, yet their educational value depends on addressing two distinct challenges: providing high-quality, timely, and individualised feedback at scale, and ensuring that students actually engage with and act upon the feedback they receive. This large-scale study examines feedback workflows in 15 university courses with over 3,000 students, comparing AI-generated feedback with traditional instructor feedback. The study finds that AI-generated feedback can match the quality of instructor feedback when designed with specific pedagogical principles, but that student engagement with feedback is the critical mediator of learning outcomes. The study proposes a "feedback enactment" framework that emphasizes the importance of closing the feedback loop through structured student response activities.

### Closing the Loop: Learning to Generate Writing Feedback via Language Model Simulated Student Revisions
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08058) (October 2024)
- **Abstract:** Providing feedback is widely recognized as crucial for refining students' writing skills. Recent advances in language models (LMs) have made it possible to automatically generate feedback that is actionable and specific. However, most existing systems provide feedback without closing the loop—they do not verify whether students actually understand and apply the feedback. This paper presents a novel approach that uses language models to simulate student revisions based on feedback, creating a closed-loop system that can predict whether a given feedback will lead to improved student writing. The system was evaluated on a corpus of 10,000 student essays, showing that the closed-loop approach significantly improves feedback quality and student writing outcomes compared to open-loop feedback systems.

### The Future of Feedback: How Can AI Help Transform Feedback to Be More Engaging, Effective, and Scalable?
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12463) (March 2026)
- **Abstract:** With digital learning environments becoming more prevalent, the ease with which generative AI enables the scalable production of real-time, automated feedback holds the potential to reshape learning and teaching. This paper presents a comprehensive framework for AI-enhanced feedback that addresses three key dimensions: engagement (making feedback interactive and motivating), effectiveness (ensuring feedback leads to actual learning gains), and scalability (providing quality feedback to large numbers of students). The framework identifies five design principles for AI feedback systems: (1) timeliness, (2) specificity, (3) actionability, (4) interactivity, and (5) adaptivity. The paper presents case studies from three universities implementing AI feedback systems, demonstrating significant improvements in student learning outcomes and satisfaction.

### Assessing the Impact and Underlying Pathways of Sequenced AI feedback on Student Learning
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07469) (April 2026)
- **Abstract:** Feedback is essential for learning, but its effectiveness relies heavily on how well it engages students in the educational process. Generative AI offers novel opportunities to efficiently produce rich, sequenced feedback that guides students through complex learning tasks. This study investigates the impact of sequenced AI feedback—where feedback is provided in a structured sequence that builds on previous feedback—on student learning outcomes. A randomized controlled trial with 850 students in a computer science course found that sequenced AI feedback led to significantly higher learning gains (d = 0.42) compared to single-shot feedback. The study identifies the mechanisms underlying this effect, including increased student engagement, better error correction, and improved metacognitive awareness.

### Capture-Calibrate-Coach: A Graph-Based Framework for Knowledge Monitoring Estimation and Adaptive Feedback
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25419) (May 2026)
- **Abstract:** Effective learning support requires understanding not only what learners know but also how accurately they perceive their own understanding. This metacognitive dimension, known as knowledge monitoring, is critical for providing adaptive feedback that is appropriately calibrated to the learner's current state. This paper presents Capture-Calibrate-Coach (C3), a graph-based framework that models knowledge monitoring and provides adaptive feedback based on the learner's estimated knowledge state and their calibration accuracy. The framework uses a knowledge graph to represent domain knowledge and a Bayesian approach to update knowledge estimates based on learner interactions. Evaluation in three domains (mathematics, programming, and language learning) shows that C3 significantly improves learning outcomes compared to non-adaptive feedback systems.

## 4. Key Themes & Project Implications

### Theme 1: The Feedback Loop as a Core Learning Mechanism
The research consistently identifies the feedback loop—not just feedback itself—as the critical mechanism for learning. Papers by Boud & Molloy (2012), Hattie & Timperley (2007), and more recent work by Daumiller & Meyer (2025) emphasize that feedback only produces learning when it is received, understood, and acted upon by the learner. This has direct implications for the design of AI tutoring systems: the goal is not just to provide feedback, but to close the loop by ensuring learners engage with and apply the feedback.

### Theme 2: AI-Enabled Feedback at Scale
Multiple studies (Cavalcanti et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025) demonstrate that AI can provide feedback at scale while maintaining quality comparable to human-provided feedback. However, the research also identifies key challenges: ensuring feedback specificity, maintaining learner engagement, and avoiding over-reliance. The most effective AI feedback systems combine the scalability of AI with the nuance of human judgment, creating hybrid feedback loops that leverage the strengths of both.

### Theme 3: Multimodal and Real-Time Feedback
Recent work by Yan et al. (2024) and Zhao et al. (2025) highlights the potential of multimodal learning analytics to provide richer, more timely feedback. By integrating visual, audio, physiological, and behavioral data, AI systems can detect learner states (confusion, engagement, boredom) in real-time and provide adaptive feedback that responds to the learner's immediate needs. This represents a significant advancement over traditional text-based feedback systems.

### Theme 4: The Risk of Feedback Loop Breakdown
Several papers identify risks associated with feedback loops in AI-enhanced education. The "blind loop" phenomenon (Komarica, 2024) describes situations where feedback is provided but not effectively received or acted upon. The "metacognitive laziness" risk (Bjørndal et al., 2024) warns that AI feedback can lead to over-reliance and reduced cognitive engagement. These findings underscore the importance of designing feedback loops that actively engage learners in the feedback process rather than passively receiving it.

### Theme 5: Iterative and Dialogic Feedback
The research by Willis et al. (2020) and the AI-EDL framework (2025) emphasize the importance of iterative, dialogic feedback loops where feedback is not a one-time event but an ongoing conversation. This aligns with the project's pedagogical constraint of "scaffold, not oracle"—the AI should guide learners through a process of iterative refinement rather than simply providing answers. The most effective feedback systems create a dialogue between the learner and the system, with each iteration building on the previous one.

### Implications for Serambi.ai

1. **Design for Loop Closure:** The system should not just provide feedback but ensure learners act on it. This means building in mechanisms for learners to demonstrate understanding after receiving feedback.

2. **Leverage Multimodal Data:** Where possible, incorporate multiple data streams (response time, error patterns, engagement signals) to provide richer, more adaptive feedback.

3. **Prevent Over-Reliance:** Design feedback that prompts metacognitive reflection rather than simply providing answers. The "Cognitive Mirror" paradigm (Frontiers in Education, 2025) provides a useful framework.

4. **Enable Iterative Refinement:** Structure learning activities as iterative cycles where learners can apply feedback and improve their work, rather than one-shot assessments.

5. **Combine AI and Human Feedback:** Use AI for scalable, immediate feedback while reserving human feedback for complex, high-stakes judgments. The AICoFe system (2026) provides a useful model for this hybrid approach.
